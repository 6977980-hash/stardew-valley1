Maximizing Your Stardew Valley Farm: A Data-Driven Approach with Stardew Tools

Introduction

Stardew Valley is a game of gentle charm and deep complexity. On the surface, it’s about planting crops, raising animals, and befriending the townsfolk. Underneath, it’s a finely tuned optimization puzzle. Every seed you plant, every machine you build, and every animal you buy has a gold-per-day value that can be calculated. For many players, the joy comes from making the most of each season, hitting milestones efficiently, and watching the gold roll in.

But the information needed to optimize is scattered. The wiki provides raw numbers, but it doesn’t answer the question you actually have at 6:00 AM on Spring 8: “I have 4,200 gold, 30 free tiles, and 6 kegs. What should I plant today?” That’s where Stardew Tools comes in. It’s a free collection of calculators and planners built around real decisions, not just data. No sign-up, no ads, just answers.

In this article, I’ll walk through how I use Stardew Tools to plan my farm, avoid common mistakes, and save hours of mental math. Whether you’re a min-maxer or a casual player who just wants to stop second-guessing, these tools can help.

Why Optimize?

Some players argue that optimizing ruins the cozy vibe of Stardew Valley. I disagree. Optimization doesn’t mean rushing or stressing. It means removing uncertainty. When you know that planting cauliflower instead of potatoes on Spring 8 will earn you more gold by the end of the season, you can plant with confidence and then go enjoy the mines or the fishing minigame. You spend less time in menus and more time playing.

Moreover, the game’s economy is designed to reward planning. Crops have different growth times, sell prices, and regrowth cycles. Machines multiply value but take time. Animals produce daily but require feed and care. The interactions are complex. A good calculator cuts through the complexity and lets you focus on the fun.

Deep Dive: Crop Profit Calculator

This is the tool I open first every in-game morning. You input the current day, your available gold, the number of free tiles, and how many kegs or jars you own. The tool then recommends one crop and explains why it wins. It even generates a shopping list.

Let’s walk through a real example. It’s Spring 8, Year 1. I have 4,200 gold and 30 free tiles. I own 6 kegs. My farming level is 3, and I chose the Tiller profession (which I regret, but that’s another story).

Without the tool, I would default to potatoes. They cost 50g, grow in 6 days, and sell for 80g. That’s a profit of 30g per potato, or 5g per day. With 30 tiles, I’d spend 1,500g on seeds and earn 2,400g, netting 900g over 6 days. That’s 150g per day.

But the tool tells me to plant cauliflower. Cauliflower seeds cost 80g, grow in 12 days, and sell for 175g. Profit per cauliflower is 95g, or about 7.9g per day. With 30 tiles, I’d spend 2,400g and earn 5,250g, netting 2,850g over 12 days. That’s 237.5g per day. That’s a 58% increase over potatoes.

The tool also considers that I have 6 kegs. Cauliflower can be turned into pickles, which sell for 2 × base price + 50. So a cauliflower pickle sells for 400g. But pickles take 2 days to process. With 6 kegs, I can process 6 cauliflowers every 2 days, which is 3 per day. Over the 12 days, I can process 36 cauliflowers, but I only have 30. So all 30 can be processed. That means each cauliflower becomes a pickle worth 400g instead of 175g. The total revenue becomes 30 × 400 = 12,000g. Subtract seed cost 2,400g, net profit 9,600g over 12 days, or 800g per day. That’s incredible for Spring Year 1.

The tool accounts for all of this. It tells me to plant cauliflower, buy 30 seeds (cost 2,400g), and use my 6 kegs to process them. It even reminds me that I need to harvest on Spring 20 and then process. This level of detail is why I trust it.

Potato 50g 80g 5g 5g (not processed)
Cauliflower 80g 175g 7.9g 26.7g (as pickles)

The difference is staggering. Without the tool, I would have left thousands of gold on the table.

Deep Dive: Keg vs. Preserves Jar Calculator

I used to build kegs and jars in a 50/50 split because it “felt balanced.” That was a mistake. The Keg vs. Preserves Jar calculator takes your specific crop and tells you which machine earns more, and how to split your harvest between the machines you actually own.

The math is simple but easy to get wrong. Kegs take longer (e.g., 7 days for wine) but multiply value by 3 for wine, 2.25 for juice, etc. Preserves jars are faster (2 days for pickles/jams) but multiply by 2 + 50. For high-value crops like starfruit, wine is far more profitable. For low-value, high-volume crops like blueberries, jam might be better because you can process more in the same time.

Let’s say I have 100 ancient fruit and 20 kegs and 10 jars. Ancient fruit wine sells for 2,310g (with Artisan profession). Ancient fruit jam sells for 1,150g. Wine takes 7 days, jam takes 2 days. In 7 days, a keg produces 1 wine (2,310g). A jar produces 3.5 jams (3.5 × 1,150 = 4,025g). So per machine, jars are actually more profitable for ancient fruit! But wait, you need to account for the number of fruit. If you have limited fruit, you want to maximize profit per fruit, not per machine. Per fruit, wine gives 2,310g, jam gives 1,150g. So wine is better if you have enough kegs to process all your fruit. If you have more fruit than kegs can handle, you might put the excess into jars.

The calculator helps you find the optimal split. For my 100 ancient fruit, with 20 kegs and 10 jars, over 7 days: 20 kegs process 20 fruit into 20 wines = 46,200g. The remaining 80 fruit can go into jars. 10 jars process 10 fruit every 2 days, so in 7 days they process 35 fruit (10 × 3.5). That leaves 45 fruit unprocessed. So I should build more kegs. The calculator tells me exactly how many kegs I need to process all my fruit: 100 fruit / 7 days = 14.3 fruit per day. Each keg processes 1/7 fruit per day, so I need 100 kegs? No, that’s not right. Actually, to process 100 fruit in 7 days, I need 100 kegs if each takes 7 days. But I can stagger. Over a season, it’s different. The tool does the math for you.

This is why I love it. It turns a complex logistics problem into a clear answer.

Deep Dive: Animal Profit Calculator

I love my chickens. I do not love the fact that I had been selling eggs raw for two years. The animal profit calculator shows gold per day for every farm animal, raw or processed, with hearts, mood, and professions factored in.

Let’s take a chicken with 5 hearts, happy mood, and the Coopmaster profession. A large egg sells for 95g (with Rancher? Actually, let’s use base). Mayonnaise from a large egg sells for 285g (with Artisan). A mayonnaise machine takes 3 hours. So one chicken produces one large egg per day. If you process it, you get 285g. If you sell raw, you get 95g. The difference is 190g per day per chicken. With 12 chickens, that’s 2,280g extra per day. That’s a lot.

But the calculator also considers that you need mayonnaise machines. Each machine processes one egg every 3 hours, so one machine can process 8 eggs per day (24/3). For 12 chickens, you need 2 machines (since 12/8 = 1.5, round up to 2). So you need to invest in machines. The tool tells you the break-even point.

For cows, cheese is better than raw milk. For goats, goat cheese. For pigs, truffles are already valuable, but truffle oil is even better. The calculator shows all this.

I used it to decide whether to buy more chickens or more cows. It turned out that with my current setup, chickens with mayonnaise were more profitable per tile than cows with cheese, because cows need more space. So I built another coop instead of a barn. That decision alone increased my daily income by 30%.

Deep Dive: Greenhouse Planner

I used to dread Winter because I didn’t know what to do with my greenhouse. The greenhouse planner lets you choose sprinklers and crops, see the layout, and project a full year of profit. It also tells you how many kegs you need to keep up with the greenhouse output.

I set up ancient fruit in the greenhouse for the first time last year. The planner told me I needed 24 kegs to process the harvest at full capacity. I had 11. I built the rest before the first harvest, and the difference in Year 2 income was massive.

Here’s why: The greenhouse has 120 tiles. With sprinklers, you can plant 116 ancient fruit. Each ancient fruit produces one fruit per week. So 116 fruit per week. Each keg takes 7 days to process one fruit into wine. So you need 116 kegs to process all fruit in a week? No, because you harvest once per week. If you harvest 116 fruit on Monday, you need 116 kegs to process them all by next Monday. But you can stagger. Actually, ancient fruit in the greenhouse produces every 7 days. So if you plant all at once, you get 116 fruit every 7 days. To process 116 fruit in 7 days, you need 116 kegs. That’s a lot. But you can plant in staggered batches. The planner helps you optimize the layout and the number of kegs. I ended up with 24 kegs because I planted in 4 batches of 29, harvesting every 2 days or so. The planner showed me that 24 kegs was enough to keep up with a staggered harvest. That’s the kind of insight that saves you from overbuilding.
